Insurance restoration

Wind damage hides in plain sight.

Wind doesn't always tear shingles off. A gust gets under the tab, breaks the sealant bond, lifts it, and lets it drop back with a hairline crease. From the ground the roof looks fine, but the seal is broken, water gets under, and the damage spreads.

  1. 01 A gust gets under Wind pushes beneath the shingle tab.
  2. 02 The seal breaks The adhesive bond lets go at the edge.
  3. 03 It lifts and flaps The tab raises, exposing the deck.
  4. 04 Crease, then creep It falls with a crease; water gets in.

A few compromised shingles after one storm can become a much larger problem, and a much larger claim, than it looks from the driveway.

Connecticut homeowners

You may be entitled to more than a patch.

Connecticut's "matching law" says that if a covered loss damages part of your roof and new shingles can't reasonably match the undamaged ones in quality, color, or size, your insurer must replace enough to restore a reasonably uniform appearance, not just patch the damaged section. In practice, a relatively small area of storm damage can entitle you to a far more complete repair.

This is educational, not a guarantee. Matching outcomes depend on your policy and the adjuster. The best way to know what you're entitled to is a free inspection, let's find out together.
